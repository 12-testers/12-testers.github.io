Google Play Policy Explained

Why is 12 Testers for 14 Days Required and How Does It Ensure Production Access?

Google Play requires personal developer accounts created after November 2023 to complete a mandatory closed test with at least 12 testers Google Play verified users opted in continuously for 14 days. This policy ensures every new Android app is tested on real hardware before reaching the public Play Store.

However, simply having 12 people click your opt-in link is not enough. Google's automated checks track whether your 12 testers for closed testing actually keep the app installed for all 336 hours, open it on different days, and leave private feedback. If even one tester uninstalls on Day 12 and your count drops to 11, your 14-day timer resets to Day 0.

By using 12-testers powered by AppConsoleLab, you eliminate counter resets completely. We assign 25 to 50 real testers on physical Android devices (Android 7 through Android 16), monitor daily engagement, and deliver a complete testing track record ready for production approval.

Day 14 Approval

Passing Google Review & The 7 Questionnaire Prompts

Once your 12 testers for 14 days cycle finishes, Google asks 7 questions before granting production access. Publishing a mid-test update (v1.0.1) plus clear answers is the #1 approval trigger.

1. How easy was it to recruit testers for your app? Explain how you organized a structured cohort of real Android users across diverse devices.
2. How did testers engage with your app during closed testing? Detail daily feature usage, screen navigation, and crash-free session verification.
3. What feedback did you receive from your 12-testers? Summarize the UI/UX suggestions and private Play Store feedback submitted by our testing team.
4. What changes did you make based on tester feedback? Reference the mid-test update (e.g., v1.0.1) you uploaded to fix layout or performance issues.
